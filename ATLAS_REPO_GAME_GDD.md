# Atlas Repo Game

## 1. One-Liner

Atlas Repo Game is a Telegram idle tycoon where players help build a real AI/SaaS ecosystem in public, turning their activity, support, learning, and promotion into access, reputation, perks, and future TON-compatible utility.

## 2. Core Idea

Atlas Repo Game is not just a game and not just a donation page. It combines:

- an idle tycoon game;
- Kirill's personal founder story;
- a launcher for AtlasRepo, Want2View, Fabric Bot, video agents, and future products;
- a loyalty and access system;
- a transparent crowdfunding layer;
- an educational platform;
- a future tokenized utility economy;
- a public YouTube/X/Telegram series reflected inside the game.

The project fantasy:

> We are building a digital factory of AI products. Players enter the control room, help operate the ecosystem, and earn a stronger place inside it as it grows.

More precise product definition:

> A game operating system for digital projects, assets, people, and money, built around the real life of founders and powered by FabricBot infrastructure.

Product roles:

- FabricBot is the financial engine.
- AtlasRepo is the knowledge base and opportunity catalog.
- The game is the living world where people see, use, upgrade, fund, and distribute everything.

FabricBot stays infrastructure:

- payments;
- TON;
- referral tracking;
- subscriptions;
- access delivery;
- balances;
- promo codes;
- partner accruals;
- digital goods.

The game should not feel like "FabricBot Game" or a payment dashboard. FabricBot works under the hood when a user buys a Founder Pass, unlocks a subscription, receives a promo code, or earns partner balance.

## 3. Positioning

The safest and strongest formulation:

> You do not buy a token hoping it will grow. You secure your place inside a growing ecosystem and unlock more rights, products, and opportunities as it develops.

Short brand line:

> Own your place in the ecosystem.

Important distinction:

- Kirill and partners keep legal ownership of products, IP, revenue, and companies.
- Players accumulate ecosystem position: access, reputation, privileges, utility, status, voting rights, and product benefits.

This should be framed as participation, support, access, and contribution, not as investment income, dividends, passive yield, or legal equity.

Honest launch position:

> Atlas Repo Game is a game-based crowdbuilding platform around a real ecosystem of products. People support projects with money, time, attention, and knowledge. They receive concrete value now, while future digital ownership mechanics are formed only after the ecosystem has enough real products, infrastructure, demand, and utility.

The project should not pretend that a finished investment model already exists. It also should not hide the larger ambition:

> We want to eventually reach a form of digital asset that reflects the real value of the ecosystem: products, code, audience, data, knowledge, infrastructure, and community.

This is stronger than launching a token first and trying to invent its purpose later.

## 4.2 Navigation As World Objects

The biggest visual risk is building a normal SaaS dashboard with a pixel room in the background.

Navigation should feel like part of the world:

- not "Assets" tab, but an asset cabinet or terminal;
- not "Referrals" tab, but Network Terminal;
- not "Funding" tab, but a strategy table with project map;
- not "Profile", but a character ID card;
- not a product list, but computers and portals;
- not a user directory, but Network City map.

Visual direction:

- dark technological room;
- slight brutalism;
- isometric 2.5D;
- soft monitor light;
- glass and metal;
- restrained neon accents;
- live shadows;
- monitors with real interfaces;
- a few high-quality animations;
- startup bunker / digital laboratory feeling.

Suggested visual language:

- physical assets: more realistic;
- game resources: glowing;
- real money/support: strict and calm;
- reputation: status-like;
- partner mechanics: network lines and connections;
- AtlasRepo: green research interfaces.

Palette:

- near-black graphite;
- cold dark blue;
- milky white text;
- AtlasRepo green;
- warm orange for real money/support;
- purple for Compute and digital resources;
- gold only for rare statuses.

Do not make everything colorful at once.

## 18. Support Language

Avoid "donate" as the default because it sounds like charity.

Avoid "invest" because it creates expectations of repayment, ownership, profit, and securities.

Use middle-ground wording:

- Support;
- Back this mission;
- Fund development;
- Join the launch;
- Become an early backer;
- Build with us;
- Activate Founder Pass.

Clear explanation:

```text
Your support funds real products and infrastructure.
In return, you receive ecosystem benefits, access, and permanent participation history.
This is not currently an equity or profit-sharing investment.
```

The interface may resemble an investment dashboard, but the product must not pretend to be an investment product.

## 22. Tokenization Direction

Do not rush to "tokenize Kirill" as the actual economic object. It is a strong phrase, but the tokenized object must have clear utility.

Possible future objects:

### Access Token

One token used across services:

- subscription payments;
- discounts;
- Credits;
- higher limits;
- launch access;
- voting.

This is the most understandable first token direction.

### Ecosystem Token

Represents participation in the whole system:

- AtlasRepo;
- Want2View;
- agents;
- education;
- community;
- content.

This needs a clear link to real product demand to avoid becoming pure speculation.

### Project Launch Assets

Separate launch assets for Want2View, Video Agent, or other products.

This avoids mixing different products, but makes the economy more complex.

### Compute Token

Users buy and spend Compute on real operations:

- research;
- channel analysis;
- generation;
- editing;
- agents.

This has measurable cost and practical utility.

### Reputation Token

Non-transferable contribution asset.

It cannot be bought on the market, but unlocks:

- voting;
- discounts;
- tasks;
- roles;
- access to new products.

Most likely future base:

1. Utility Credits: bought and spent.
2. Contribution Reputation: earned and non-transferable.

A real ecosystem token can be considered later on top of those two primitives.

## 23. Empty Token Narrative

The game can say this directly:

```text
We are building the project economy in public.
```

Brand promise:

## 25. Core Gameplay Loop

The base loop:

```text
Enter -> collect resources -> choose project -> perform action -> improve project -> earn reputation and perks -> unlock new opportunities
```

### Passive Actions

Players assign virtual agents to computers:

- AI Research Agent;
- Content Agent;
- Sales Agent;
- GitHub Scanner;
- Trend Scanner;
- Video Agent.

Agents generate game resources while the player is away.

### Active Actions

Active tasks should be short and sometimes genuinely useful:

- choose the better project from two options;
- evaluate an AI tool;
- complete a short lesson;
- test a product feature;
- leave feedback;
- guess which trend will grow;
- choose the better title or thumbnail;
- vote for the next feature;
- complete a quest;
- share a project;
- invite a user.

### Real Ecosystem Tasks

Some actions help real products:

- AtlasRepo shows three repositories and asks the player to categorize them.
- Want2View shows previews and asks which is more clickable.
- A new product asks the player to test onboarding.
- Content tasks ask users to vote on hooks, titles, or thumbnails.

These tasks must be optional, verified, and spam-resistant.

## 41. Visual Direction

The first screen should be the actual game, not a marketing landing page.

Style goals:

- polished Telegram Mini App;

## 42. What Not To Say In MVP

Avoid:

- "equity";
- "investment income";
- "dividends";
- "token will grow";
- "invest now and earn later";
- "company percentage";
- "passive income";
- "revenue share" unless legal structure is ready.
- "token allocation" unless officially defined.
- "guaranteed return".

Prefer:

- Support;
- Participation;
- Contribution;
- Access;
- Benefits;
- Ecosystem Capital;
- Project Power;
- Founder Privileges;
- Utility;
- Rewards;
- Credits.
